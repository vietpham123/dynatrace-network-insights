Network Insights · Before you deploy

Four decisions to make first

Read this before installing anything. Three of these are easy to change later. One is not, and getting it wrong costs you entities you cannot delete. None of them require touching Dynatrace — they are questions about your network and your organisation.

~30 minutes no tooling needed read before install

Why this exists as its own document

Once the app is installed it guides the rest of the deployment itself — it can see your tenant, so it knows what is configured and what is not. These four questions come before that, which is the one place a document can tell you something the product cannot.

1. Does any part of the estate reuse IP address space?

Irreversible · answer before onboarding anything

This is the only decision here that cannot be undone.

Device identity is the management IP address. Two devices that share an address — routine after a merger, and normal for DR pairs and Kubernetes meshes — collapse into a single entity.

And entities are permanent: the delete API returns 405, and they do not age out. Entities with 90+ days of zero data are still listed. So a wrong answer here is not something you clean up later — it is something you live with.

Ask your network team

"Is there anywhere in the estate — any acquired site, any DR pair, any lab — where two devices could have the same management IP?"

No overlapProceed. This is the common case for a single well-run estate.
Overlap existsIt has to be resolved in the network before onboarding — NAT at the interconnect, or VRF-unique management addressing. There is no setting for this.
Nobody is sureTreat that as yes. Scope the first deployment to one site you can vouch for, and widen once you know.

To be explicit: overlapping address space is not supported

Entity identity is the management address and nothing else — network_device_{device.address}. There is no scope, tenant or ActiveGate-group qualifier that lets two devices share an address, and adding one would be a change to the extension rather than a configuration option. Do not plan a deployment on the assumption that separate ActiveGate groups keep overlapping estates apart. They do not.

Topology is the exception, and it is the reason this gets misremembered. Adjacency joins on the LLDP chassis ID, not on IP, precisely because overlapping ranges are legitimate — so link discovery survives overlap perfectly well. Device identity does not. The two behave differently and only one of them is safe.

2. How large is the first deployment?

Reversible · but it shapes the first month

Scope it to a site. A few hundred devices is comfortable.

A whole large estate is not, yet — and this is a product limit today, stated plainly rather than discovered in front of your team:

AreaWhere it stands
Roster and liveness queriesUnbounded — no row limit
Fleet tableNo row virtualisation; every row is in the DOM
TopologyCaps at 400 edges, with a visible truncation banner
Topology layoutReadable to roughly 12 nodes per tier; unreadable near 60

At tens of thousands of interfaces the Fleet and NetFlow pages would attempt to render the whole estate.

Decide with your team

"Which single site do we start with — one that is representative enough to prove the value, and small enough to render?"

Deploying one app per branch does not help

Site membership lives in app state, not in the metrics, so each instance would still query the whole estate and filter in the browser — N× the load for the same result. It also breaks the cross-site views (hub↔branch, WAN topology, fleet-wide hotspots) that justify the app in the first place. Multi-app is a tenancy argument, not a scale one.

3. What will it cost to run?

Reversible · but easier to plan than to unwind

Metrics are predictable. Flow is the lever.

SNMP metric ingest with 15-month retention is straightforward to size — it scales with interface count and poll interval, both of which you control. Your Dynatrace account team can size it precisely from your interface count.

NetFlow is the variable one. Flow arrives as logs, and log volume is what moves the bill. It is also the highest-value data in the product — it is what answers "who is talking to whom" — so the question is not whether to collect it but how much.

FullEvery flow record. Highest fidelity, full per-interface drill-down. The default.
SampledKeep 1-in-N, for very high-rate links. Volumes are extrapolated from each exporter's stamped sampling rate.

You pick this when you set up the collector, and you can change it afterwards. The app's look-back picker defaults deliberately tight to bound query cost as well.

Ask your Dynatrace account team

"Here is our interface count and our expected flow volume — what does that mean for our licence?"

4. Who needs to approve this, and what will they ask for?

Reversible · but it is the usual cause of delay

Start this early. It is almost always the long pole.

Two approvals are typically needed, and neither can be granted while you wait:

ApprovalWhat to ask forWho
Install the app The app-engine:apps:install permission on the account doing the install Your Dynatrace administrator
Run the app app-engine:apps:run for the team, plus the metric and log read permissions they most likely already hold Your Dynatrace administrator

A security reviewer will usually ask three things. All three have concrete answers:

What can the app do?Exactly the permissions listed in app.config.json, each with a comment saying why. Nothing outside that list is possible.
Where do credentials live?The Dynatrace Credential Vault. The app references entries by id and never reads a secret.
Who signs the code?You do. Extensions are signed with your own certificate authority — nothing runs that you did not sign.
Send ahead of the install session

"Here is the permission list and the source. Can we get app-engine:apps:install granted before Thursday?"

What you need in place on the day

An ActiveGate

Required

A host inside your network that can reach your devices over SNMP. The app's setup guide walks through installing one.

It does the polling and runs the extensions. Nothing reaches your devices from the Dynatrace side.

A read-only SNMP credential

Required

v2c community or v3 credentials, for the devices in your first site.

Read-only is sufficient for everything the product does with SNMP.

Somewhere to build

Required

A machine with Node.js 16.13+ and Python, able to reach the public npm and PyPI registries.

Check the registry access ahead of time. Corporate proxies block it more often than anything else, and it stops the session before it starts.

A certificate authority

Created on the day

One clean CA for signing extensions. You generate it during setup — nothing to obtain in advance.

One per project, and keep the vault clean: a broken or expired CA there can stop every custom extension loading.

Then what

With those four answered, the order is short:

Install the app

Follow the install walkthrough — the one document you need before the app can guide you itself.

Assign sites and roles as you onboard

During onboarding, not after. Site scoping is the whole scale strategy, and Unassigned is the one bucket no view can help with.
